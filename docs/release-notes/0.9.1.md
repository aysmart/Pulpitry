## The Song Finder

The catalogue is here. It is downloaded once, after the subscription is
activated, and searched on the machine from then on — by meaning, so a line
half-remembered or a description of what a song is about finds it.

- **Searching needs nothing of the network.** The query is embedded by the model
  Pulpitry already carries and matched against the catalogue in under a
  millisecond, so it works with the network unplugged in the middle of a set,
  and the words typed never leave the building.
- **An interrupted download is safe.** It resumes rather than restarting, and a
  pull that never finishes leaves whatever was there before.
- **Without a subscription the pane is locked** and says so, with the way to
  activate one. Nothing is asked of the network while it stands.

Every song carries what is known about its rights, including "not established",
which is the honest answer for a great many of them and is said rather than
hidden.

## The line under the words

A desk that had never been given a look of its own took its caption setting
from the wall — and the wall wears the look of whichever desk last put
something on it. So once a chorus had been up, the song desk's answer (no
titles, and off by default since 0.9.0) became the scripture desk's answer
too: the reference under the verse was gone, on the panes and on the screen,
with nothing connecting the two.

Each desk now decides for itself. The colours, the type and the background
still come from the wall; whether there is a line underneath does not.

**And the switch in the look editor works.** Applying a look from the shelf
deliberately keeps the desk's own answer, so that choosing a background cannot
turn titles on. But the editor contains that switch — ticking it, pressing Save
and watching nothing happen taught people the setting was broken. A look that
has just been edited is applied whole.

## Which look you are on

A thin gold ring around one tile among thirty-five, each of them full of
colour: on a bright look the gold sat on gold. The one in use is now ringed,
offset from the grid by a gap of its own ground, and labelled **In use** — any
two of the three can fail and the answer is still not in doubt.

It is also judged on everything the look owns rather than on the caption, which
belongs to the desk. Otherwise no tile could ever match on the song desk, where
the answer is no and every shipped look says yes.

## Song Finder says what it is waiting for

The pane described a feature, had no search box, and said nothing about the
absence — which reads as broken software rather than as a shut door.

- **Without a subscription** it carries a padlock and a button that opens the
  subscription page. Nothing is asked of the network while it stands.
- **While the catalogue is still being assembled** it says *Coming soon*, and
  will start working on its own when it arrives. There is nothing to install.

## A slow connection costs a few words, not every word after them

Reported as "transcription lags from time to time". A connection that cannot
carry the microphone used to queue the audio instead — and audio is realtime,
so there is no idle moment later to work a backlog off in. A three-second stall
therefore cost three seconds on **every** utterance for the rest of the service.

Audio is now dropped rather than queued when the connection falls behind, so
the transcript stays level with the room. Nothing skipped is ever billed.

## Reconnecting means reconnecting

- **A heartbeat that could not reach the subscription** said "reconnecting" and
  nothing ever took it back, so one blip left the desk reading as down for the
  rest of a service that was working perfectly. It says so once now, and says
  it is listening again when the connection returns.
- **A reconnection resumed five seconds behind the room.** The audio held during
  a handshake kept the oldest five seconds; it now keeps the newest two.
- **The commonest failure — one dropped connection** — waited 1.2 seconds
  before anything was attempted. The first retry is now a quarter of a second,
  with each further one waiting longer.

## Imports

Two things a library built by pasting carries with it, now taken off at the
door — for every import, not only the ones we make packs from.

- **A lyrics page's credit line** — "1 ContributorHow Great Thou Art Lyrics" —
  arrives glued to the first word of the first verse, with no blank line and no
  label to break on. The opening line of the song, which is what a search
  matches and what goes on a screen, was a contributor count.
- **An annotated heading** — "[Verse 1: BeBe & CeCe Winans, BeBe, CeCe]" — is a
  heading with the singers named on it. Read whole it was too long to be a
  label, so the song opened with a list of performers.
