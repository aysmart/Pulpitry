## See what each output is sending

Displays now draws a thumbnail beside each output's controls, using the same
views that output's own window runs: its look, its Bible, its capitals, and
whatever is live at that moment.

An output with no screen of its own could not be looked at before. The only way
to see what it was sending was a receiver on another machine — which is how a
feed that was announced and blank went unnoticed for two days.

- **An overlay is drawn over a chequer**, because what it sends is furniture on
  nothing at all, and a black box would read as a black screen.
- **With nothing live, a sample verse stands in**, marked as one: choosing
  between six shapes should not mean choosing between six empty boxes.
- **A clip is named rather than played.** Five thumbnails playing a service's
  audio would be worse than no preview.

It is drawn at the desk rather than captured from the output's window, so it
shows what an output should be drawing rather than proving what a switcher
receives. The row's "on the network" line, and Check the network can see it,
are the parts that answer that.

## Gone

**"No projector open — open one"** has left the top bar. With every screen in
Displays, the projector is one output among several, and its being closed says
nothing about whether the congregation has a screen.
