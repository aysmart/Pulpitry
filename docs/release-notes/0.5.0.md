Thayer's and the Greek behind every verse, a desk rearranged around what you are
actually doing, and definitions you can put on the wall a sense at a time.

## The original languages, beside the chapter

The **Original** panel sits next to the library and has whatever verse you have
staged, word by word: the Greek or Hebrew, how to say it, and what each word
means in that place.

Press any Strong's number and the **Lexicon** tab beside it opens on the entry —
the Translators Brief lexicon for both testaments, and **Thayer's** for the Greek
New Testament. 425,424 words and 20,192 entries, bundled like the twenty-two
translations, read with no network and no account.

**The definitions go on the wall a paragraph at a time.** A preacher expounding a
word wants one sense of it, and the numbered senses are exactly how the entries
are broken up — so every paragraph is its own target. Click stages it,
double-click sends it. The whole article is offered too, as the second option
rather than the only one.

The word itself projects as three lines: the original, how to say it, what it
means. Never the Strong's number and never the grammar code — those are how you
know you have the right word, not what a congregation needs to read.

## Anything taller than the screen now winds

A lexicon entry is several hundred words. It was either clipped or shrunk to a
size nobody past the third row could read.

Now the type stays the size your theme asked for and you wind through it with the
arrows beside Display, which say where you are — *3 of 7*. They appear only when
there is something to wind, which for a verse or a chorus is never.

**A paired Castavox follows you through it**, to the same words rather than to
the same frame number: the two lay the text out differently and cut it into
different numbers of frames.

## The desk, rearranged

**Live has the whole column.** Preview and Live were stacked, which gave each of
them half the height and put a look-ahead level in weight with the thing the
congregation is actually reading.

**Preview moved beside the library that fills it.** Choosing a verse and checking
the result were on opposite sides of the screen. The bottom row now reads left to
right in the order you work: the library, the original languages, what is staged
next.

**Preview shows the real thing.** It draws your theme — the backdrop, the
typeface, the colour — where before it drew plain text on a panel.

**Everything is 16:9**: the projector, the second display, Preview and Live. They
disagreed before, and each showed a different number of lines of the same words,
so the pane labelled Live was not showing what was live.

## Themes

**New** starts a theme from the defaults. Adjust opens on what is on the
projector and Copy opens on somebody else's preset, so building a look for your
own room meant starting from one of those and undoing it.

**The reference beneath the words has its own typeface, size and colour.** It is
a different thing being said: the verse is what the congregation reads, "John
3:16 (KJV)" is a citation, and a service sheet or a broadcast lower third would
happily set it in another face or your church's accent colour. Left alone it
follows the scripture exactly as it always did.

## Smaller things you will notice

- A queue entry goes live on a **double-click**, as everything else on the desk
  does.
- **Clear** on the Detected panel and on the Queue, and a **×** on any queue row
  to take one out.
- The queue rail shows the running order beside you rather than behind a tab.

## Telling us this installation exists

New, **off**, and it stays off unless you switch it on in Settings.

Once a day it can send a random identifier made on your machine, which
application and version, your operating system, what kind of processor it has,
and which transcription engine you chose. Your country is worked out from the
connection and the address is discarded.

It does not send this machine's name. It does not send your address. It does not
send a transcript, a verse, a reference or a filename — there is no field in it
for any of those. If it cannot be sent, nothing happens and nothing is retried.

Our privacy policy says that on the free local engine nothing leaves the
building. For anybody who leaves that switch alone, that is still exactly true.
