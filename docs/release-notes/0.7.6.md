Summaries work for a service of any length, and say what is wrong when
something is.

## A four-hour service can be summarised

A church teaching for four hours produced a 27,000-word transcript, and the
summary failed. It reported that the assistant could not be reached, which sent
them to check a network that was working, and advised trying again — at
something that would have failed identically every time.

Long services are now read in overlapping stretches and the readings drawn
together into one summary. Nothing is left out. What this replaces sent the
first 30,000 words and the last 15,000 and told the model the middle had been
dropped: for an afternoon's teaching that is most of the teaching, and a summary
of the first and last hours is not a summary of the service.

The summary now grows with the service, too. A forty-minute sermon and a
five-hour session no longer get the same budget, and the longer one is given
room for a section on each movement of it.

## When it will not, it says why

Every refusal used to arrive as "the assistant could not be reached". The
assistant had been reached; it had read the whole request and declined it. Now
the reason reaches you, and the reason decides what happens next:

- **Too long for the model** — asked again in smaller pieces, automatically.
- **Blocked by the model's safety filter** — the service is split so that only
  the stretch it objected to is affected. That stretch is named in the summary
  and the rest is summarised. A summary with a stated gap is worth having.

Scripture is now cited rather than reproduced. A summary names the passage and
says what was drawn from it, which is what a reader with a Bible actually needs
— and it avoids a copyright filter that was blocking whole summaries for
quoting translations at length.

## Add a service from a transcript file

**Add from file…** in Past services takes a `.txt` transcript and adds it as an
ordinary past service. Summaries, exports and slides all work on it exactly as
they do on a service heard live.

For a service recorded before Pulpitry, one taken down by hand, or one recorded
on a different machine from the one you work on. A transcript exported from
Pulpitry is understood as what it is: the header and the timestamps come off, so
what is stored is what was said.

## Also in this release

A brief lexicon entry no longer breaks across two screens. Short entries were
being set at the largest size available and overflowing a frame they would have
fitted comfortably — so they paginated precisely because they were short. The
size is now measured rather than guessed. Long entries still wind, unchanged.
