Every translation used to ship whether a church wanted it or not. Now there is a
library to choose from, a Catholic Bible in it, and you can tell Pulpitry what to
do without reaching for the keyboard.

## A scripture library, not a fixed set

Settings, under Scripture, now holds the library: what is installed, what else
there is, about a megabyte to add one and the disk back when one goes.
Thirty-three translations in nine languages — the ones churches actually read
from, grouped by language, each saying what it costs before you spend it.

Ten come installed rather than twenty-two. The ten are the King James and the
World English Bible plus every non-English text, so Pulpitry still works with no
internet at all in whatever language your church reads. What is no longer
bundled is English alternatives, and they are a few seconds away.

A translation you remove stays removed. It used to come back on the next launch.

## A Bible a Catholic parish can use

The Catholic Public Domain Version: seventy-three books in the order a parish
reads them, with Tobit and Judith after Nehemiah, Wisdom and Sirach after the
Song of Solomon, Baruch after Lamentations and Maccabees after Malachi.

Every Bible we had carried sixty-six, our own Douay-Rheims included, so the
lectionary would have failed on an ordinary Sunday in Ordinary Time.

## Telling it what to do

Say "next verse", "previous verse", "clear the screen", or the name of a
translation — "give me the New King James" — and it happens. It has to be the
whole of what you say, so a reading that mentions the next verse leaves the
screen alone. A wake word is there if you want a stronger guarantee for a phrase
you expect to hear from the pulpit, and your own words can be taught: "shema"
can mean next verse.

Off until you turn it on, in Settings under Audio.

## Fixed

Changing translation now changes what is staged, and what is on screen. It used
to change only what the next lookup would search, and leave the old wording in
front of the congregation.

The text licences several translations require us to show have moved from About
into Help, beside the other things this application owes and to whom.

Removing the translation you were reading no longer leaves Pulpitry pointed at a
Bible that is not there.
