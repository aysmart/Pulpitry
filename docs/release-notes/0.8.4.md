Two engines, and a model of your own choosing.

## Two engines, not three

The transcription engine is now **This machine** or a **Castavox
subscription**. Streaming through an Azure Speech account of your own has been
withdrawn.

If that is what you were using, this update moves you to **This machine** — not
to a subscription, because we will not put you on a paid engine over a decision
we made. A model is fetched for your hardware on first launch if you do not
already have one, and the Azure key you gave us is deleted from your credential
store: we asked for it, we no longer use it, and we have no business keeping it.

What you lose, said plainly: word-by-word streaming without paying us. This
machine transcribes a sentence once it has ended, so a verse arrives a beat
later. If that beat matters in your service, a subscription is what buys it
back.

Our terms said transcription through your own Azure account was part of the
free software "and always will be". That sentence is withdrawn in the same
release as the engine, and the terms now say so rather than quietly losing it.

## The assistant reaches whichever model you have

The assistant — the part that suggests passages a speaker paraphrases, and
writes the summary afterwards — was addressed to Azure AI Foundry alone. It now
reaches essentially any model, chosen from a list rather than typed:

Ollama or LM Studio **on your own machine**, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini,
DeepSeek, Grok, Mistral, Groq, OpenRouter, Azure OpenAI, or anything else that
speaks one of the three shapes. Choosing a provider fills in the address; the
model name stays yours to type, because a list of model names is wrong within a
month.

Not supported, deliberately: Amazon Bedrock and Google Vertex. Neither
authenticates with an API key — one wants request signing, the other a service
account — and neither is a field in a settings pane. Use OpenRouter, or run the
model locally.

Nothing about this touches transcription, which happens on this machine either
way. A small model on a church laptop gives a rougher summary and takes longer;
that is the model, not a fault.

## The engine moved to Account

Because it is the question of what this costs. Choosing a subscription reveals
the licence field and nothing else; choosing this machine reveals the
transcription model and, under it, the model of your own. Neither church reads
past the other's settings to find its own.

The switch that turns paraphrase suggestions on stays under Scripture, next to
the matching it changes.

## Test, before a service rather than during one

A **Test** button on the model settings. It makes one real request and tells you
which step failed — reached, key accepted, model found, answered. There was no
way to learn any of that except to start a service and watch nothing happen.

It tests what is on the screen rather than what is saved, so you can check a new
address before committing it.

## Plain HTTP reaches your own network

A model on the one PC with a graphics card, serving the laptop at the desk, is
the obvious arrangement and was refused: plain `http://` was allowed to this
machine only. It now reaches anywhere on your own network. The public internet
is still refused, because there the request crosses equipment nobody in the
church controls.

What you are choosing: the transcript crosses your own wiring unencrypted.

## Spoken languages

The language table loses its Azure column with the engine. Swahili, Korean,
Amharic, Arabic, Mandarin, Tagalog, Indonesian, Ukrainian, Romanian and Tamil
were Azure-or-local, and are now local-only — the subscription's engine never
heard them.

**Zulu can no longer be transcribed at all.** Azure was the only engine that
could. It stays in the list saying so, rather than disappearing from under
anyone who had chosen it. The Zulu Bible is unaffected and can still be
searched, typed and displayed.

## Fixed

A refused connection to your own model no longer blames the internet. Pointing
the assistant at LM Studio and being told to check a connection that was working
perfectly, and that nothing in the request had used, was the wrong advice at the
worst moment. It now says nothing answered at that address, and that an
application being open is not the same as its server running.
