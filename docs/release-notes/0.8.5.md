Every screen its own, a screen for the platform, and a deck that follows the
sermon.

## Display outputs

**Displays**, beside Settings, lists every congregation-facing screen. The
projector and the second display are simply the first two, and everything an
output can be told now reaches them too.

Every screen shows the same verse at the same moment — two screens disagreeing
about what is live is the one failure this cannot have. What differs is each
screen's own:

- **The look** it draws in, from your saved themes.
- **The translation** it reads. A main wall in one Bible and an overflow room in
  another, from one reference — each room in the wording it can follow. The
  reference line names the translation actually on that screen.
- **The shape**: the whole screen, or an overlay.
- **Capitals or sentence case.** Capitals for a short band read in a glance;
  sentence case for a screen carrying more than a line, where capitals are
  measurably slower to read.
- **Where it goes**: a monitor, or none at all and only the network.
- **The format** a switcher expects — 1080p or 720p, 30 or 60, or 4K.

On the first launch after this update, the projector has no screen until you
choose one. Guessing a monitor would put a congregation's display somewhere
nobody chose.

## A keyable overlay, not a picture of a screen

An output can be sent over NDI as **furniture on nothing at all**, with its
transparency intact, so a switcher lays it over the camera instead of receiving
a photograph of a projector. Six shapes: lower third, ribbon, corner card, top
strip, side panel, and captions with no band. Each takes its colours and
typeface from that output's theme; an overlay with nothing live draws nothing,
rather than an empty band.

Each network feed is around 100 Mbit/s. Three is a third of a gigabit — a
wired-network feature, and worth knowing before Sunday rather than during it.

## A screen only the platform sees

Give an output the shape **Stage display** and it shows what nobody in the
congregation should: a timer counting up, or down to the minute you set, the
time of day, what is on the congregation's screen, **what comes next in the
plan**, and a line the desk can send the platform — "five minutes", "wrap up".

Switch off the clock and the live text and it becomes the timer alone, as large
as the screen allows, readable from anywhere on a platform. Over time turns red.

## Following the sermon through your slides

Stage a deck and press **Follow**. Pulpitry says which slide the preaching has
reached, as a button you may press rather than something done to you. **Move by
itself** is there too, off, and says plainly what it does.

It weighs the order a deck is actually presented in — a slide just ahead is
where a speaker goes, one six back is not — because a deck about one sermon
reads alike to any similarity measure, and order is information the words do not
contain.

## Slides by voice

"Next slide", "previous slide", "slide four". They walk the deck that is staged,
exactly as the Next button does, and never the service plan: nothing said about
slides should reorder a service.

## Backgrounds

**Blur**, **scale**, and **how it meets the frame** — fill and crop, show all of
it with bars in your theme's colour, or stretch. Blur is the tool the dim was
standing in for: dimming makes a photograph darker, blurring makes it stop
competing.

## Your subscription

- **The address on the account** is shown under the plan, so you can tell which
  subscription this machine is on.
- **Renew this subscription** appears when one has ended. It opens the
  subscription page in your browser and sends a sign-in code to that address —
  no licence key to find.

## Fixed

- **A machine moved to a new licence kept showing the old subscription.** It
  received a fresh key for the plan it had just left, so its hours and renewal
  date were the wrong subscription's. Fixed on our side; a machine already
  showing the wrong one corrects itself when its licence is entered again.
- **Turning NDI on for the first time said the projector window was missing**
  when the real answer was macOS asking for Screen Recording permission. It now
  says that.
- **Additional displays showed what was live when they opened and never changed
  again.** They could not be told anything after opening.
- **A long verse ran off the bottom of the stage display.**
