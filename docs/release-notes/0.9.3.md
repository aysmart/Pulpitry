Everything an operator asked for after a service, and verse numbers set properly.

## Compare Scripture

A passage in several translations side by side, or two different passages
against each other — John's account beside Mark's. Open it from the scripture
desk with a passage staged.

Add a translation and its column appears beside the others. Add a passage and
it joins them, checked before it is added so a reference that does not resolve
tells you at once rather than putting an empty column on a screen. Where every
column is the same verse it is set out verse against verse; where they are
different passages, each column runs on its own.

Send it to the screen as a slide, which is a table the congregation can read
rather than three blocks of prose. Your translations are remembered between
services; the passages are not, because the passages are this Sunday's.

## Verse numbers, raised

A passage of several verses can be numbered, and the switch for it is in
Display settings — "Number the verses in a passage". A single verse is never
numbered: its reference is already underneath it.

The numbers are now set as superscript everywhere: the wall, your Preview and
Live panes, the stage display, the lower thirds and the shapes your church drew
in the studio. A number set in line with the words reads as part of the
sentence — "16 For God so loved" begins with sixteen — which is the one thing a
verse number must not do.

## Finding a verse faster

- **Books offered as you type.** The book picker is a box you type at: three
  letters and Enter. Any part of the name matches, so "cor" reaches
  1 Corinthians without knowing it is filed under "1", and punctuation is
  ignored — "1co", "1 co" and "i cor" all arrive at the same place. The
  translation picker works the same way.
- **Suggestions under the search box.** Type two letters of a book name and the
  books that match appear as chips under the box; click one to go there.
- **Enter once to preview, twice to go live.** Anywhere on any desk. The second
  press has to follow the first — press Enter, look at Preview, press it again.
- **Extend a passage with Shift and an arrow.** Shift + ↓ adds the verse below
  to what is staged, Shift + ↑ the verse above, and reversing shortens it
  again. Ctrl works the same way.

## A sheet of shortcuts

Press **?** for every key this desk answers to, grouped by the job rather than
by the key — because nobody comes asking what B does, they come asking how to
take the screen down. Six new ones: ⌘F to the search box, Space to step on,
B to black the screen, [ and ] to wind a long passage, and 1–9 to jump to a
desk. None of them fire while you are typing.

## Screens

- **An output set to "None — on the network only" no longer opens a window on
  your laptop.** It sends over NDI and nothing else, which is what it says.
- **Closing Pulpitry closes the screens.** Quitting used to leave the
  congregation's window on the wall and the NDI feeds running.

## Around the desk

- Every button at the top of the window now has an icon **and** a word:
  Displays, Themes, Tour and Settings. Three of them were icons alone and had
  to be hovered to be told apart.
- **"Looks" is now called Themes** — the button, the screen it opens and the
  picker on each output.
