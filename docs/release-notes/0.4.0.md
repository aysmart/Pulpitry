The release where Pulpitry stops asking you a question you could not answer.

## Transcription on your own machine is now what it opens with

A fresh install used to open on Azure, which meant it opened on a demand for a
subscription key — a paywall standing in front of the thing that is free.

It now opens on the engine that runs on your own computer. No account, no key,
no network, no hours to buy, and no limit on how long you use it. If it keeps up
on your hardware, that is the whole of what you need from us.

**If you are already using Azure or a Castavox subscription, nothing changes.**
This is for new installs only. Your engine, key and region are exactly where you
left them, and an upgrade will not move you.

## And it chooses a speech model for you

The local engine used to open on a list of about thirty model files, from 30 MB
to 1.5 GB, and would not let you save until you picked one. Nothing in that list
told you whether *your* laptop keeps up with `base` or with `small`, so it was a
question with no honest answer — and the free engine sat behind it, unusable,
looking broken.

Pulpitry now measures the machine and picks one, and downloads it in the
background when you first open the app rather than on a Sunday morning when you
press Listen. You can still change it under Settings whenever you like.

The guess deliberately errs small. Too small costs a little accuracy on names,
which is quiet and easy to correct; too large costs a transcript that arrives
after the sentence, which the whole congregation sees.

And when the machine genuinely cannot keep up, it now names the model that
would, rather than telling you to go and choose a smaller one — which was the
same unanswerable question asked twice.

## Words appear sooner on a fast machine

The local engine transcribes the sentence as it is being spoken, as well as when
it ends, and that is what lets a verse be found before the speaker has finished.
It used to do it on a fixed 2.2-second beat, chosen for the slowest machine
imaginable.

It now measures how long a pass actually takes on your hardware and goes as
often as that allows — as little as 600 ms apart on a fast laptop. On a slow one
nothing changes: it was already skipping these when it was busy, and it still
does.

## One less thing to fill in on a subscription

The Settings dialog used to refuse to save until a speech model was chosen —
including when you were on a Castavox subscription, which does not use one at
all.

## Behind the scenes

Transcription for subscribers can now run on a second speech service. Which one
your session uses is decided by us, per session, and nothing about it is
configured on your machine — but a copy of Pulpitry this old or older stays on
the one it knows, so an update is never something you have to hurry.

## Installing

Not code signed. macOS will refuse to open it until you right-click and choose
Open; Windows SmartScreen will warn.

The engine that runs on your own machine needs no Node runtime. Azure and
Castavox subscriptions still need Node 20 or later, which is not bundled.
Scripture detection, the projector, songs and everything else work without it.
