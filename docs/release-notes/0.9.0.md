One window, arranged four ways — and a tour of the whole of it.

## Four desks

Everything an operator needs has always been on screen at once. In a hurry that
is the whole point; to somebody opening Pulpitry for the first time it was a
wall of panels with no way in.

So the window is arranged for the job in hand, and shows only the panels that
job needs.

- **Scripture** — what is being said, what was heard in it, the feed of verses
  shown and verses set aside, the Bible, the original languages, and the
  service plan.
- **Songs** — the library, the set the band is playing, and the parts of the
  staged song.
- **Slides** — a deck read as thumbnails, the library that stages it, and the
  studio behind both.
- **Media** — pictures and clips, with a clip's transport under the live pane.

What is on the wall stays exactly where it is when you move between them, and
each desk stages its own item, so getting the next song ready while a verse is
up costs nothing. Live is one thing, because there is one wall: it wears the
look of whichever desk put something on it.

## Show me around

The first launch walks through the window: a light on one panel at a time and a
sentence about it, forty-five stops through the four desks and the three screens
behind them — the outputs, the looks and the settings, which it opens rather
than pointing at.

It changes nothing. It stages nothing, saves nothing and presses nothing inside
a screen, and it puts you back on the desk you started from however it ends.
The question mark in the top bar starts it again whenever somebody new sits
down.

## A studio for slides

A slide can be laid out by hand: text, headings, shapes, pictures, tables and
scripture boxes, positioned against the frame they will be seen in.

- **Nine shapes**, with gradients, and colours that carry transparency
  throughout.
- **Every font on the machine**, read from the system rather than a list of
  five.
- **A deck carries its own background**, so each slide is designed against the
  thing it will be shown on.
- **Slides drafted from a sermon open in the same studio** and are edited the
  same way. What the engine wrote is a starting point rather than a separate
  kind of slide.

## Shapes a church draws itself

A stream's furniture is the most church-specific thing on any screen: their
band, at their proportions, in their colours, usually with their logo in it.
Display shapes is a studio for exactly that, and a library to keep them in,
with twenty-one templates to start from — including the six forms Pulpitry has
always drawn, now as templates a copy of which is yours to move.

A shape draws when it has something to put in it, and a slide is a whole screen
rather than a line of a band: an overlay output lets slides pass and keeps
showing the camera.

## Songs as they were written

A song is kept as its writer broke it and split into screenfuls at the moment
it goes up — Auto, or a fixed one to ten lines. The title under the words is
off: nobody singing needs to be told which song they are singing.

- **Imports arrive sectioned**, with the author kept, from EasyWorship,
  ProPresenter, OpenLP and OpenSong.
- **The set is not the service.** Two people build them, often on different
  days; reordering the set cannot reorder the service.
- **Song Finder is marked Coming soon** while the catalogue is assembled. The
  pane starts working on its own when it arrives.

## Three lists, three lifetimes

The service plan is what the service is going to do, saved and reopened next
Sunday. The Queue is a passage set aside because the preacher said they would
come back to it, and ends with the service. The song set is what the band is
playing. They were one list, so a preacher doubling back three times left three
verses in Sunday's running order.

Every desk can add to the plan, and the button beside it fills that desk's own
list. Both are named after the list they fill.

## Looks, per desk

Each desk keeps its own, because a verse and a chorus are not read the same
way. The looks screen is a library of them, drawn at the size they will be read
from, with twenty motion backgrounds that are drawn rather than played from a
file — so they cost nothing to keep and do not pixelate on a wall.

Applying a look no longer carries the title switch, which is a decision about
the desk rather than about the look.

## Smaller things that were getting in the way

- **A reference typed the way it is said.** "Jere 32 11" now lands on the verse
  and stages it, rather than the chapter with the verse to be found by
  scrolling. Spaces and commas separate as readily as a colon.
- **Enter stages, and a second Enter puts it up** — the keyboard's half of the
  rule every list already followed.
- **A clip keeps its place** when you look at another desk, and a projector
  opened mid-clip joins where the room is.
- **An output that lands on the screen you are working on** keeps its pointer
  and says that Esc closes it, rather than looking like a crash. Escape now
  closes the screen it was pressed on rather than a different one.
- **Displays notices what changed while it is open** — a screen plugged in, a
  look saved, a shape drawn.

## Gone

**The Plan desk.** A running order is written and then followed, and the
following happens from whichever desk holds the next thing. It is a tab beside
the readings now, where a service is actually planned.

**Six overlay forms as the only answer.** They are templates in the shape
library instead, and an output pointed at one still draws exactly what it drew.
