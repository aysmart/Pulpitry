A long service is summarised without losing the work when the model gets busy,
and Pulpitry says what it is doing while it does it.

## Waiting, instead of starting over

A four-hour service is read in several parts — one request each, and one more to
draw them together. On an ordinary deployment that is enough requests in enough
seconds to exhaust its per-minute quota, and 0.7.6 gave up when it did.

A 27,000-word service got through all three parts and was refused on the last
request of the four. Every part had been read; all of it was thrown away. Trying
again did the same thing, because trying again immediately is the one thing that
cannot work.

Pulpitry now waits and carries on, for exactly as long as the service asks where
it says so.

## Saying what it is doing

That makes a summary something that can run for minutes, and minutes of silence
look like a hang. Past services now shows which part is being read — "Reading
part 2 of 3…" — so a pause reads as waiting rather than as a crash.

If it has not finished within ten minutes it stops and says so, naming how many
parts it managed. That is the difference between a deployment that is marginally
too small and one that is hopeless, and it is worth knowing which you have.

## Summaries that were blocked now arrive

A summary quoting scripture at length can be refused by the model's copyright
filter. When that happens Pulpitry asks again with quoting forbidden and
paraphrases instead. The wording is a little flatter; the alternative was no
summary at all.

## Also in this release

Pulpitry no longer crashes when it cannot write to its own log. Writing a log
line could fail, failing raised an error, and reporting that error tried to
write another log line — which took the whole application down mid-service. A
log line that cannot be written is not worth an application.

The times in the log are the times on your clock. They were an hour out in
British summer time, which makes a log actively misleading rather than merely
unhelpful.
