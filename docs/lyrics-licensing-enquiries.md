# Licensing the song catalogue: three enquiries to send

Drafts, ready to send once the two bracketed figures are filled in. Send all
three the same week — the answers only make sense against each other, and the
one question that decides everything is the same in each: **how much Nigerian
gospel do they actually hold?** A Western catalogue with 40 million songs and
no Sinach, Nathaniel Bassey or Dunsin Oyekan is not a catalogue for this
market.

Figures to fill in before sending:

- `[INSTALLS]` — churches running Castavox or Pulpitry today.
- `[TERRITORIES]` — where they are, in order of size.

---

## 1. LyricFind

**To:** licensing@lyricfind.com (or the enquiry form at lyricfind.com)
**Subject:** Lyric licence for church presentation software — Nigeria and UK

Hello,

I build Castavox and Pulpitry, desktop software that churches use to put
scripture and song lyrics on the screen during a service. We have `[INSTALLS]`
churches using it, mostly in `[TERRITORIES]`, and we are looking to license
lyrics properly rather than ask our customers to type them in.

What we would need:

- Lyrics for **on-screen projection inside the application**, during a live
  church service — not consumer playback, no audio, no sync.
- Either a catalogue we ship with the app or a search-and-fetch the operator
  uses before the service; whichever your licence permits.
- **Offline availability for a song once fetched.** Our customers' internet
  fails mid-service regularly, which is the single most important constraint we
  have.
- Credits shown on screen: title, writers, copyright line, and the church's own
  CCLI licence number where they hold one.

My questions:

1. Does your catalogue cover **Nigerian and West African gospel** — for
   example Sinach, Nathaniel Bassey, Dunsin Oyekan, Tope Alabi — and
   Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa language repertoire? This is the part that decides
   whether a licence is useful to us.
2. How is such a licence priced: per church per month, per request, or a flat
   catalogue fee?
3. Is offline caching on the church's machine permitted, and for how long?
4. What attribution do you require on screen?
5. Is there a minimum term or minimum spend for a company of our size?

Happy to send a build so you can see exactly how lyrics would appear.

Thank you,
Ayorinde G. Smart
castavox.com

## 3. CCLI

**To:** support@ccli.com, and the UK office if they redirect
**Subject:** SongSelect integration for a church presentation app — is it open?

Hello,

I build Castavox and Pulpitry, desktop presentation software for churches —
`[INSTALLS]` churches, mostly in `[TERRITORIES]`. Our customers hold CCLI

I understand the SongSelect API partner programme is closed to new partners. My
questions:

1. Is there any current route for a new presentation app to integrate
   SongSelect, so that a church **signs in with its own CCLI account** and
   imports the songs its licence covers?
2. If not, is there a waiting list, or a different programme for smaller
   vendors?
3. Does CCLI's licensing reach **Nigerian gospel repertoire** and
   Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa language songs? Our churches ask for those first, and I
   want to know whether a CCLI route would serve them or only Western worship
   music.
4. Separately: we show a church's CCLI licence number and each song's copyright
   line on the projected screen. Is there a form of words or placement you
   would prefer?

We would rather do this the way you would want it done than build around you.

Thank you,
Ayorinde G. Smart
castavox.com

## Reading the answers

- **If any of them covers Nigerian gospel and permits offline display**, take
  it: that is the "install it and your songs are there" feature, defensible,
  and it is a line on the pricing page a competitor cannot copy without the
  same deal.
- **If the catalogues are Western-only**, the licence is worth less than the
  public-domain local-language hymnals we can ship ourselves — and that
  changes the order of the plan rather than the plan.
- **If CCLI opens SongSelect**, it is the best of the three: the licence is the
  church's own, the songs are the ones they already report, and we carry no
  catalogue at all.
